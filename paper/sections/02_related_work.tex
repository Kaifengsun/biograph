\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

FDA and ICH sources connect supply continuity to manufacturing quality and risk management \cite{fda2019shortages,ichQ9R1}. The openFDA interface adds machine-readable shortage fields and harmonized identifiers \cite{openfdaShortages}. These records support reproducible fact checks, but a database field does not establish the regulatory interpretation attached to it. FDA quality-systems guidance and EMA Annex 11 illustrate the complementary textual layer \cite{fdaQualitySystems2006,ecAnnex11}.

BM25 uses term frequency, document frequency, and length normalization \cite{robertson2009bm25}. Dense passage retrieval maps queries and passages to a shared vector space \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}. Exact regulatory identifiers favor lexical matching, whereas paraphrases may favor dense models. Generated hypothetical text can support zero-shot retrieval \cite{gao2023hyde}, but generated profiles must resolve to a source passage before they can count as evidence.

We test two neural alternatives. Qwen3-Reranker scores query-passage pairs after BM25 retrieval \cite{zhang2025qwen3embedding}. MedCPT provides query and article encoders plus a cross-encoder trained from PubMed search logs \cite{jin2023medcpt}. MedCPT is more retrieval-specific than biomedical entity encoders, so it gives a direct test of semantic transfer without task-specific fine-tuning.

Hierarchical retrieval can expose document-level context and recursive summaries \cite{sarthi2024raptor}. Knowledge graphs preserve typed entities, relations, and provenance \cite{hogan2021knowledgegraphs}. Our design assigns them different responsibilities: hierarchy helps navigation, while graph paths verify structured claims. Neither representation automatically proves that a passage answers a question.
