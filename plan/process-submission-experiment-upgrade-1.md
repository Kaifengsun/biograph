# Introduction

![Status: In Progress](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-In%20Progress-orange)

This plan converts the PharmGraphRAG project from a prototype paper draft into a submission-oriented research package suitable for a credible Scopus-indexed journal or EI/Scopus/CPCI-indexed international conference. The target is not a course paper. The target is an independently first-authored paper that can support overseas graduate school applications by October 2026 through one of the following evidence states: accepted conference paper, journal under review, journal revision invited, or journal accepted.

The core publication claim is deliberately conservative: PharmGraphRAG improves evidence-grounded regulatory and supply-chain risk reasoning through domain-specific GraphRAG and agentic simulation. The paper must not overclaim direct drug-shortage prediction unless leakage-free simulation evidence supports that claim.

## 1. Requirements & Constraints

- **REQ-001**: The final paper must be suitable for a legitimate international journal or conference indexed by at least one of Scopus, EI Compendex, CPCI, Inspec, CNKI, or Google Scholar.
- **REQ-002**: The preferred target is a credible Scopus-indexed journal with transparent peer review, visible CiteScore/SJR metadata, and no active discontinued-source warning.
- **REQ-003**: The backup target is a credible EI Compendex, Scopus, or CPCI conference with verifiable prior proceedings indexing in the official database or by the proceedings publisher.
- **REQ-004**: The paper must be independently first-authored by the student.
- **REQ-005**: By 2026-10-01, the project should reach at least one application-usable status: submitted, under review, revision invited, conference accepted, or journal accepted.
- **REQ-006**: All formal experimental results must be reproducible from frozen corpus, frozen evaluation set, frozen scripts, and saved result artifacts.
- **REQ-007**: Retrieval experiments must report Recall@5, Recall@10, Recall@20, MRR, NDCG@10, evidence precision, latency, and LLM call count.
- **REQ-008**: Simulation experiments must remove historical-outcome leakage before any result is described as predictive or historically faithful.
- **REQ-009**: The paper must distinguish prototype demonstrations from validated empirical claims.
- **REQ-010**: Results generated by automatic keyword ground-truth construction may be used only for debugging, not for the final paper tables.
- **CON-001**: Do not rely on unimplemented baselines in the paper. Any baseline named in Section 5 must be implemented, run, and saved.
- **CON-002**: Do not submit the same full manuscript to multiple venues at the same time.
- **CON-003**: Any destructive data operation, including Neo4j `DETACH DELETE`, file deletion, index dropping, or canonical data replacement, requires explicit user confirmation under `CODEX_RULES.md`.
- **CON-004**: The current corpus is inconsistent: `data/chunks` contains 2,410 enriched chunks, FAISS contains 3,500 document vectors, and Neo4j contains 4,016 `DocChunk` nodes according to `HANDOFF.md`.
- **CON-005**: Current simulation results cannot support strong prediction claims because `agent_sim/simulator.py` uses `event.actual_shortage_weeks` during weekly state evolution.
- **GUD-001**: Use "historical calibration and scenario analysis" as the simulation framing unless leakage-free predictive evaluation is completed.
- **GUD-002**: Prefer a smaller number of fair baselines over a larger table of nominal baselines.
- **GUD-003**: Ask Nektarios Oraiopoulos for feedback after the experiment design and first reliable results are available, not after the entire paper is polished.

## 3. Alternatives

- **ALT-001**: Target SCI/SSCI Q1 or Q2 immediately. This was not chosen as the primary 2026 application strategy because review timelines are too uncertain for October 2026 evidence, and current experiments need substantial strengthening before supporting that level of claim.
- **ALT-002**: Target a fast Google Scholar-only outlet. This was rejected because such venues provide weak application value and may damage credibility if the outlet is low quality.
- **ALT-003**: Submit quickly using current experimental results. This was rejected because current retrieval ablation results show no difference across A/B/C/D variants, and current simulation contains historical-outcome leakage.
- **ALT-004**: Implement many external GraphRAG baselines such as GraphRAG-Global, LightRAG, HippoRAG, and Document-GraphRAG before submission. This remains optional because incomplete or unfair baseline implementations create more review risk than a smaller set of well-implemented baselines.
- **ALT-005**: Frame the paper as drug-shortage prediction. This was rejected as the default framing because the existing evidence more strongly supports regulatory evidence retrieval, reasoning, and scenario analysis than direct forecasting.

## 7. Risks & Assumptions

- **RISK-001**: Current data inconsistency may require a user-approved Neo4j cleanup or corpus rebuild before formal evaluation.
- **RISK-002**: QAPair generation may incur API cost and may not improve retrieval; use pilot evaluation before full corpus generation.
- **RISK-003**: Full GraphRAG with LLM-guided walk may be slow or unstable for 80 to 100 queries; cache retrieval outputs and track failures explicitly.
- **RISK-004**: Leakage-free simulation may reduce apparent performance. If so, the paper should emphasize scenario analysis and decision support rather than predictive superiority.
- **RISK-005**: Scopus-indexed journals vary widely in quality; poor venue choice may reduce application value.
- **RISK-006**: Some conferences advertise EI or Scopus indexing without guaranteed post-publication coverage; only official or prior-proceedings verification is acceptable.
- **RISK-007**: Advisor feedback may require reframing from operations management toward information systems, AI decision support, or healthcare supply-chain risk.
- **RISK-008**: Review timelines may extend beyond October 2026 even for suitable journals.
- **RISK-009**: Source metadata can be wrong even when the document downloads successfully. Run source audit and prompt-grounding QA before corpus freeze.
- **ASSUMPTION-001**: The user prefers a credible publication trajectory over a fastest-possible low-quality acceptance.
- **ASSUMPTION-002**: The project can spend June and July primarily on experiment repair and validation before heavy prose polishing.
- **ASSUMPTION-003**: The current local environment remains available with `D:\Anaconda3\python.exe`, Neo4j, FAISS, sentence-transformers, and existing project data.
- **ASSUMPTION-004**: The advisor feedback course can provide useful positioning comments before venue submission.
